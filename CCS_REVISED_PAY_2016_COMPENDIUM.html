Foundation

What the 2016 Rules actually say

The Rules were notified on 25 July 2016 and are deemed to have come into force on 1 January 2016. They replace six earlier revision-of-pay rule-sets, apply to persons appointed to civil posts in connection with the affairs of the Union whose pay is debitable to the Civil Estimates, and fix pay in a matrix of 18 levels printed across 19 columns — Level 13A sits between Levels 13 and 14.

Scope and exclusions — Rule 2

Rule 2(1) applies the Rules to persons appointed to civil services and posts in connection with the affairs of the Union whose pay is debitable to the Civil Estimates, and to persons serving in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department. Rule 2(2) carves out eight categories:

  1. Group A, B and C posts under the administrative control of the Administrator of the Union Territory of Chandigarh;
  2. persons locally recruited for service in Diplomatic, Consular or other Indian establishments in foreign countries;
  3. persons not in whole-time employment;
  4. persons paid out of contingencies;
  5. persons paid otherwise than on a monthly basis, including piece-rate payment;
  6. persons employed on contract, except where the contract provides otherwise;
  7. persons re-employed in Government service after retirement; and
  8. any other class of persons whom the President may, by order, specifically exclude.

The Explanatory Memorandum clarifies that Railways, Defence Services personnel paid from Defence Services Estimates and Gramin Dak Sevaks are outside these Rules because separate rules govern them, whereas work-charged establishments are covered, since Rule 2 does not exclude them.

Source: Rule 2 and the Explanatory Memorandum to G.S.R. 721(E).

Switchover — Rule 5 and Rule 6

Rule 5, first proviso: an employee may elect to continue drawing pay in the existing structure until he earns his next or any subsequent increment there, or until he vacates the post or ceases to draw pay in that structure. Second proviso: where he was placed in a higher grade pay between 01.01.2016 and the date of notification on promotion or up-gradation, he may elect to switch over from the date of that promotion or up-gradation.

Rule 6: the option must be exercised in writing in the prescribed form within three months of notification — by 25 October 2016 — and must reach the Head of Office along with the undertaking in the form appended to the Rules. Rule 6(4): the option once exercised is final.

Rule 6(3): if the intimation is not received in time, the employee is deemed to have elected the revised pay structure from 01.01.2016. And by Rule 5, Explanation 2, the option is not admissible at all to a person appointed to a post for the first time, or by transfer, on or after 01.01.2016.

Source: Rules 5 and 6, with Notes 1–3 to Rule 6 (terminated service, death, and leave on 01.01.2016).

Rules 8 and 9 — entry and increments

  • Rule 8: a direct recruit appointed on or after 01.01.2016 is fixed at the minimum pay or first Cell of the Level. Where pay had already been fixed in the existing structure before notification and his existing emoluments exceed that first cell, the difference is paid as personal pay absorbed in future increments.
  • Rule 9 reads, in full: “The increment shall be as specified in the vertical Cells of the applicable Level in the Pay Matrix.” The increment is therefore movement to the next cell — the Rules nowhere state a percentage or a rounding rule.
  • The cells themselves rise by about 3%, rounded to the nearest ₹100 (7th CPC report, para 5.1.38). That is a property of the printed Schedule, inferred from it, not a formula in Rule 9.
  • A Level's column ends at its last cell; below the last cell there is no further increment until promotion or up-gradation.

Source: Rules 8 and 9 (with the Rule 9 illustration: ₹32,300 → ₹33,300 in Level 4), Part A of the Schedule.

2017 amendment

G.S.R. 592(E) — what changed

  • Part A substituted in whole. The amendment replaced Part A of the Schedule outright. The substantive change is Level 13: entry pay rose from ₹1,18,500 to ₹1,23,100 and the column now has 20 cells instead of 21, closing at ₹2,15,900. The index of rationalisation for the level moved from 2.57 to 2.67.
  • Consequential instructions followed in DoE O.M. No. 4-6/2017-IC/E.III(A) dated 28.09.2017.
  • Part C additions. Items 48 to 52 were inserted after item 47 — Poster Artists, EME Corps (GP ₹2,000 → ₹2,400, Level 4), Russian Translation Officer (GP ₹4,600 → ₹5,400, PB-3, Level 10), Russian Senior Translation Officer (→ GP ₹6,600, Level 11), Russian Editor (→ GP ₹7,600, Level 12), and the Junior Works Manager pool of the Department of Defence Production (GP ₹4,200/₹4,600, with 10% earmarked for GP ₹4,800, Level 8, and non-functional up-gradation to Level 9).
  • Defined-date correction. In Rule 3(v), for the figures “2006” the figures “2016” were substituted — which matters, because “existing emoluments” carry the DA component of the fitment.
  • Nothing else changed. The notification amends only Rule 3(v), Rule 12 and the Schedule. In particular, Rule 7, Rule 9 and Rule 10 are untouched by the 2017 amendment.
  • Rule 12 widened. Pay protection by personal pay on Central deputation, earlier confined to officers under the Central Staffing Scheme, was extended to posts not covered by that Scheme, with effect from 01.01.2016.
  • The amendment is deemed to have come into force on 01.01.2016, so Level 13 pay is re-fixed from that date and arrears follow.

Source: CCS (Revised Pay) (Amendment) Rules, 2017, G.S.R. 592(E), F. No. 1-2/2016-IC, dated 15.06.2017.

Rule 13

On promotion on or after 01.01.2016: one increment in the Level from which the employee is promoted, then placement at the cell equal to that figure, or the next higher cell of the promoted Level.

Rule 14

Arrears for the period from 01.01.2016 are to be paid during the financial year 2016-17. “Arrears” means the difference between the revised and the unrevised pay plus DA for that period.

Rules 15–17

The Rules override the FRs and all earlier revision-of-pay rule-sets; the President may relax them by special order; and the Central Government decides questions of interpretation.

How the matrix is put together

Each horizontal row is a cell number; each vertical column is a Level. Within a column the printed cells rise by about 3%, rounded to the nearest ₹100, which reproduces every one of the 521 column-to-column steps in Part A. Levels are not aligned to each other — a Level 7 cell can equal a Level 8 cell, which is exactly why promotion fixation speaks of the “equal or next higher” cell rather than a fixed jump.

next cell ≈ round(previous × 1.03 ÷ 100) × 100

A reconstruction of the printed progression, not a formula prescribed by the Rules: Rule 9 simply says the increment is “as specified in the vertical Cells”.

Reading the column headings

  • Level 6 corresponds to pre-revised GP ₹4,200, into which GP ₹3,600 and ₹4,000 were merged.
  • Levels 9 and 10 both carry GP ₹5,400 — L9 is PB-2 (₹9,300–34,800), L10 is PB-3 (₹15,600–39,100).
  • Level 13A carries GP ₹8,900, which is why it needs a column of its own between 13 (₹8,700) and 14 (₹10,000).
  • Levels 15–18 are the HAG, HAG+, Apex and Cabinet Secretary scales.

The NPA complication — Rule 7(1)(B)

For medical officers to whom NPA is admissible the method changes. Sub-clause (i): multiply existing basic pay by 2.57, then add DA on the pre-revised NPA, and locate that total in the Level — identical cell, otherwise the immediate next higher one. Sub-clause (ii): the pre-revised NPA admissible on the existing basic pay is then added to the pay so fixed, until the revised rates of NPA are decided.

The Gazette illustration runs ₹21,000 × 2.57 = ₹53,970, plus ₹6,563 of DA on a pre-revised NPA of ₹5,250 (that is 125%), giving ₹60,533 and therefore ₹61,300 in Level 10.

From 01.07.2017 NPA is 20% of revised basic pay, and basic pay plus NPA may not exceed ₹2,37,500. The printed text of Rule 7(1)(B)(i) refers to DA “as on 1st day of January, 2006”; the 2017 amendment corrected that slip only in Rule 3(v), while the illustration applies the 01.01.2016 rate of 125%.

Source: Rule 7(1)(B); Rule 13(ii); DoE O.M. No. 12-2/2016-E.III.A dated 07.07.2017.

Pay protection — Rules 7(7) to 7(11)

  • 7(7) Personal pay: where existing emoluments exceed the revised emoluments, the difference is allowed as personal pay, absorbed in future increases of pay.
  • 7(8) Stepping up on fixation: where pay is fixed in a cell lower than that of a junior in the same cadre, who was drawing less immediately before 01.01.2016, the senior is stepped up to the junior's cell.
  • 7(10) Stepping up on promotion: where a senior promoted before 01.01.2016 draws less than a junior promoted on or after that date, his pay is stepped up from the date of the junior's promotion, subject to four conditions — same cadre and identical posts, identical lower and higher structures, the senior drawing equal or more pay at the time of his promotion, and the anomaly flowing directly from FR 22 or the pay-fixation rules. The order issues under FR 27, and the next increment then falls after the required qualifying service from re-fixation.
  • Proviso: no stepping up where the junior drew more because of advance increments.
  • 7(11): officiating pay fixed lower than substantive pay is brought to the same stage.

Source: Rules 7(7)–(11); DoPT O.M. No. 4/3/2017-Estt.(Pay-I) dated 26.10.2018 (consolidated guidelines).

Rules 13 and 10

Promotion, increments and the DNI option

Promotion fixation is a two-level operation: an increment is earned in the Level left behind, and the result is then located in the Level entered. The optional FR 22(I)(a)(1) route defers that to the date of next increment and then applies two increments at once.

Rule 10 — dates that move

  • Two increment dates: 1 January and 1 July, instead of the single 1 July date earlier.
  • Appointed, promoted or granted MACP between 2 January and 1 July (both inclusive) → increment on 1 January.
  • Appointed, promoted or granted MACP between 2 July and 1 January (both inclusive) → increment on 1 July.
  • The proviso to Rule 10(1): only one annual increment, on one of the two dates. The dates are alternatives, never additive.

The FR 22(I)(a)(1) option

Within one month of promotion an employee may opt to have his pay fixed from his date of next increment in the lower post instead of from the date of promotion. The two routes then diverge:

Rule 13(ii) — the NPA ceiling on promotion

For Government servants receiving NPA, basic pay plus NPA shall not exceed the average of the basic pay of the revised scale applicable to the Apex Level and to the Level of the Cabinet Secretary — that is the average of ₹2,25,000 and ₹2,50,000, or ₹2,37,500.

Source: Rule 13(ii); DoE O.M. No. 12-2/2016-E.III.A dated 07.07.2017.

Monthly estimate

Salary estimator

Basic pay from the matrix plus DA, HRA (with the statutory floors), transport allowance and optional NPA. This gives gross emoluments before deductions such as NPS, CGHS, CGEGIS and income tax.

What is included

  • DA on basic pay at the selected rate, rounded to the nearest rupee.
  • HRA at the city-class rate, subject to the ₹5,400 / ₹3,600 / ₹1,800 floors.
  • Transport Allowance at the level-appropriate flat rate, plus DA on it.
  • NPA at 20% of basic pay, trimmed so that basic pay plus NPA never exceeds ₹2,37,500.

What is excluded

  • Deductions — NPS (10% of basic pay plus DA), CGHS, CGEGIS, professional tax, income tax.
  • Special pay, personal pay, and post-specific allowances such as hardship or uniform allowance.
  • CEA and Hostel Subsidy, which are reimbursements and depend on actual expenditure.
  • Arrears and one-off payments.

HRA is not admissible where the employee occupies government accommodation; the estimator assumes it is admissible.

CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021

Pension, gratuity and family pension

Retirement benefits flow from the last basic pay drawn in the matrix. The 2021 Rules consolidated the earlier pension rules and are the operative text for a civil servant retiring today.

Pension — Rule 44

Pension is 50% of the last basic pay, or of the average emoluments of the last ten months, whichever is higher, for qualifying service of ten years or more. The floor is ₹9,000 a month and the ceiling ₹1,25,000.

Qualifying service below ten years attracts a lump-sum withdrawal benefit instead of pension.

Source: CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, Rule 44.

Gratuity — Rule 45

Retirement gratuity is one quarter of “emoluments” for each completed six-month period of qualifying service, subject to a maximum of 16.5 times the emoluments. Emoluments here means basic pay plus DA.

With DA at 50%, the monetary ceiling rose from ₹20 lakh to ₹25 lakh with effect from 01.01.2024. Death gratuity follows a separate slab scale.

Source: CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, Rule 45; DoP&PW O.M. raising the ceiling with effect from 01.01.2024.

Family pension — Rule 51

Normal family pension is 30% of the basic pay last drawn, subject to a floor of ₹9,000 a month. Where the employee dies in service after ten years of qualifying service, the enhanced family pension is 50% of basic pay for ten years, reverting thereafter to the normal rate.

Source: CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021, Rule 51.

Commutation and NPS

Up to 40% of pension may be commuted for a lump sum, the commuted portion being restored after 15 years. Employees covered by the National Pension System are governed by the NPS Rules, 2021 rather than by Rules 44 and 45, and receive a lump sum plus an annuity from their corpus.

Check whether the employee is on the old (defined benefit) or the NPS (defined contribution) scheme — the calculator applies only to the former.

How the numbers on this page were checked

  1. Matrix. All 540 cells were compared with Part A of the Schedule, and each of the 521 column-to-column steps was re-derived from its predecessor (about 3%, rounded to the nearest ₹100). Every step reconciles, including the 20-cell Level 13 substituted in 2017.
  2. Illustrations. The three Gazette illustrations under Rule 7 were recomputed arithmetically and the resulting amounts located in the matrix — ₹32,300, ₹61,300 and ₹33,900, in Levels 4, 10 and 5 respectively.
  3. Cell counts. Levels 1–10 have 40 cells each (400); Level 11 has 39; Level 12 has 34; Level 13 has 20; Level 13A has 18; Level 14 has 15; Level 15 has 8; Level 16 has 4; Levels 17 and 18 have 1 each — 540 in total.
  4. Rule 9 wording. The Rule grants the increment as the vertical cell movement and prescribes no percentage. Statements elsewhere that “Rule 9 grants 3% rounded to the next ₹100” are a conflation of the Rule with the printed Schedule and with para 5.1.38 of the 7th CPC report; this compendium keeps the two apart.
  5. DA. The series runs from 0% on 01.01.2016 to 63% from 01.07.2026, with the freeze of January 2020 to June 2021 reflected as a restoration rather than as arrears.

Scope and caveats

  • This is an educational compilation. In an individual case the Head of Office fixes pay under the Rules, and the Central Government's interpretation under Rule 17 prevails.
  • Railways, Defence Services personnel paid from Defence Services Estimates and Gramin Dak Sevaks are not governed by these Rules; parallel rules apply to them, although the matrix is similar.
  • The compendium covers the 7th CPC structure. Any revision recommended by a later Pay Commission is outside its scope; the DA rate shown is the instalment effective 01.07.2026.
  • Allowance rates change by O.M.; where an O.M. is cited, the O.M. governs and not this page.
