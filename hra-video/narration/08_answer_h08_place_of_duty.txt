The answer is the place of duty. Paragraph 3.1 says House Rent Allowance is admissible with reference to the place of duty of the Government servant, irrespective of whether he is residing at that place or anywhere else. Paragraph 3.2 fixes the boundary. The locality is the named municipality or corporation, and it also includes suburban municipalities, notified areas or cantonments that are contiguous to it, plus any other area the Central Government notifies. Paragraph 3.3 removes any doubt about change. If a State Government includes an area within, or excludes it from, the municipality or Urban Agglomeration, these instructions automatically apply or cease to apply from that very date. So the trap is this: living elsewhere does not by itself defeat your claim. What matters is the place of duty. But remember, the separate conditions in Paragraph 5 can still bar you on other grounds. Remember: House Rent Allowance follows the place of duty, not where you sleep, and boundary changes apply automatically from the date the State Government makes them.
