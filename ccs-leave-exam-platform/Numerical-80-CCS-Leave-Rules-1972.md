## Earned leave — credit and ceiling

### Q1. A Government servant joins service on 15 April. How much earned leave stands to his credit for that first half-year under Rule 27(1)?

- (A) 7.5 days
- (B) 3.75 days
- (C) 15 days
- (D) 5 days **← answer**

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — Only completed calendar months count. April is broken, so it contributes nothing.
Step 2 — The completed months are May and June — two months.
Step 3 — Credit is 2 1/2 days per completed month: 2 × 2.5 = 5 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That would count March, April and May — three months. April is a part month and May brings it to two, not three.
- B — That halves the correct figure again; the 2 1/2 days rate is not halved.
- C — That is the full half-yearly instalment under Rule 26(1)(a). It is credited only to a servant already in service on 1 January or 1 July.

**Exam-safe point: Part month never counts, however many days were worked.**

> *Authority:* Rule 27(1) and the proviso to Rule 26(1)(a), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q2. A Government servant joins on 1 March. What earned leave is credited for that half-year?

- (A) 7.5 days
- (B) 10 days **← answer**
- (C) 12.5 days
- (D) 15 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — Service begins on the first day of March, so March counts as a full completed month.
Step 2 — Completed months in January–June are March, April, May and June — four.
Step 3 — 4 × 2 1/2 = 10 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That is three months; March to June is four.
- C — That is five months, which would include February, before he joined.
- D — The full instalment is for a servant who was already on the rolls on 1 January.

**Exam-safe point: Joining on the 1st of a month makes that month a completed month.**

> *Authority:* Rule 27(1), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q3. A servant joins on 1 January. The half-year runs to 30 June. How much earned leave is credited for that half-year?

- (A) 15 days **← answer**
- (B) 30 days
- (C) 7.5 days
- (D) 12.5 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — January to June is six completed calendar months.
Step 2 — 6 × 2 1/2 = 15 days.
Step 3 — That is the same as the ordinary instalment, which is exactly why the rule looks redundant until a mid-half-year entry or exit occurs.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- B — 30 days is the whole year's credit, not the half-year's.
- C — That is the pro-rata figure for a servant joining in April.
- D — That is five months; January to June is six.

**Exam-safe point: A full half-year of service yields exactly the 15-day instalment.**

> *Authority:* Rule 27(1), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q4. On 31 December a servant holds 290 days of earned leave. He takes 12 days of earned leave in the January–June half-year and no more. What is his balance on 30 June?

- (A) 300 days
- (B) 293 days **← answer**
- (C) 278 days
- (D) 305 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The balance of 290 is above 285 and not above 300, so the proviso to Rule 26(1)(b) applies: the 15-day advance credit is kept apart.
Step 2 — The 12 days taken are set off against that held credit, leaving 15 − 12 = 3 days to post.
Step 3 — Balance = 290 + 3 = 293 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — 300 is the ceiling, not the arithmetic. The buffer route is not a capping route.
- C — That deducts the 12 days twice — once against the held credit and again against the balance.
- D — That posts the whole 15 days and ignores the buffer, which exists precisely to stop the account reaching 305.

**Exam-safe point: Above 285: hold the credit apart, set off what is taken, post only the unspent part.**

> *Authority:* Proviso to Rule 26(1)(b), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q5. A servant holds 292 days of earned leave on 31 December and takes 8 days in February. What is the closing balance on 30 June?

- (A) 307 days
- (B) 284 days
- (C) 300 days
- (D) 299 days **← answer**

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — 292 lies in the band above 285, so the 15-day credit is held separately.
Step 2 — 8 days are drawn against the held credit: 15 − 8 = 7 days left to post.
Step 3 — 292 + 7 = 299 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That is 292 + 15, the answer the buffer exists to prevent.
- B — That deducts the 8 days from the balance after posting the credit, counting the leave twice.
- C — The ceiling does not operate here; the buffer does.

**Exam-safe point: 292 held + (15 − 8) posted = 299.**

> *Authority:* Proviso to Rule 26(1)(b), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q6. On 31 December a servant holds exactly 285 days of earned leave and takes no leave in the next half-year. What is his balance on 30 June?

- (A) 305 days
- (B) 295 days
- (C) 300 days **← answer**
- (D) 290 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The buffer engages only where the balance is 300 days or less but MORE than 285. At exactly 285 it does not engage.
Step 2 — So the ordinary credit of 15 days is posted.
Step 3 — 285 + 15 = 300 days, exactly at the ceiling.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That would credit 20 days; the half-yearly instalment is 15.
- B — That applies the buffer and assumes 5 days of leave were taken; none were.
- D — That credits only 5 days, as though the proviso applied and 10 days had been taken.

**Exam-safe point: The buffer band is (285, 300] — 285 itself draws a plain credit.**

> *Authority:* Proviso to Rule 26(1)(b), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q7. On 31 December a servant holds 286 days of earned leave and takes none in the following half-year. What is his balance on 30 June?

- (A) 301 days
- (B) 300 days **← answer**
- (C) 286 days
- (D) 295 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — 286 is more than 285 and not more than 300, so the proviso engages and the 15-day credit is held apart.
Step 2 — No leave is taken, so nothing is set off against the held credit.
Step 3 — The unspent credit is posted at the close, but 286 + 15 = 301 would breach the ceiling, so 14 days are posted and the balance is 300.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That applies the buffer and then lets the posted balance cross the ceiling, which the ceiling forbids.
- C — That forfeits the whole credit; the proviso holds it, it does not destroy it.
- D — That assumes 9 days of leave were taken; none were.

**Exam-safe point: The buffer preserves the credit; the ceiling still governs the posted balance.**

> *Authority:* Rule 26(1)(b) and its proviso, CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q8. A servant holds 288 days on 31 December and takes 10 days in the half-year. What is his balance on 30 June?

- (A) 293 days **← answer**
- (B) 300 days
- (C) 303 days
- (D) 278 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — 288 is inside the buffer band, so 15 days are held apart.
Step 2 — 10 days are set off against the held credit: 15 − 10 = 5 days to post.
Step 3 — 288 + 5 = 293 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- B — That caps the result rather than computing it.
- C — That posts the full credit and ignores the buffer.
- D — That deducts the 10 days from the balance as well as from the held credit.

**Exam-safe point: Same three steps every time: test the band, set off, post the residue.**

> *Authority:* Proviso to Rule 26(1)(b), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q9. A servant retires on 31 August. What earned leave is credited for that final half-year?

- (A) 10 days
- (B) 15 days
- (C) 5 days **← answer**
- (D) 7.5 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The half-year is July–December but service ends on 31 August.
Step 2 — Completed months up to the date of retirement are July and August — two.
Step 3 — 2 × 2 1/2 = 5 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That is four months; only two are completed by 31 August.
- B — The full instalment is for a servant who serves the whole half-year.
- D — That counts three months, which would run into September, after retirement.

**Exam-safe point: On retirement, credit runs to the date of retirement.**

> *Authority:* Rule 27(2)(a), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q10. A servant is dismissed on 15 March. What earned leave is credited for the January–June half-year?

- (A) 0 days
- (B) 5 days **← answer**
- (C) 6.25 days
- (D) 7.5 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — On removal or dismissal, credit is allowed only up to the end of the calendar month preceding the month of dismissal.
Step 2 — The preceding month ends on 28 February, so January and February are the won months.
Step 3 — 2 × 2 1/2 = 5 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — Dismissal stops the credit at the preceding month-end; it does not erase the earlier months.
- C — That counts 2 1/2 months, treating the part of March worked as a fraction. Part months never count.
- D — That includes March, which the rule expressly excludes on dismissal.

**Exam-safe point: Dismissal and removal stop at the preceding month's end; retirement and resignation run to the date.**

> *Authority:* Rule 27(2)(b), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q11. A servant dies in service on 20 November. What earned leave is credited for that half-year to his account?

- (A) 10 days **← answer**
- (B) 7.5 days
- (C) 15 days
- (D) 12.5 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — On death in service, credit runs at 2 1/2 days per completed month up to the date of death.
Step 2 — July, August, September and October are completed; November is not.
Step 3 — 4 × 2 1/2 = 10 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- B — That counts only three months, dropping October.
- C — The full instalment is not earned by a servant who dies mid-half-year.
- D — That counts November, which was not completed on 20 November.

**Exam-safe point: Death is treated like retirement for the date, not like dismissal.**

> *Authority:* Rule 27(2)(c), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q12. A servant holding 290 days of earned leave has 20 days of unavailed joining time. How much joining time is credited as earned leave?

- (A) 15 days
- (B) 20 days
- (C) 10 days **← answer**
- (D) 0 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — Unavailed joining time is creditable up to a maximum of 15 days.
Step 2 — But earned leave at credit plus joining time must not exceed 300 days.
Step 3 — 290 already held leaves room for 10; so 10 is credited and the remaining 10 is not.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That applies the 15-day cap and ignores the 300-day ceiling on the combination.
- B — Both the 15-day cap and the ceiling are breached.
- D — Joining time is creditable; it is not banned.

**Exam-safe point: Two limits bite together: 15 days, and the 300-day ceiling.**

> *Authority:* Rule 26(1)(a)(ii), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972


## Half pay leave and dies non

### Q13. A servant joins on 1 May. How much half pay leave is credited for that first half-year under Rule 29(2)(a)?

- (A) 3.33 days
- (B) 5 days
- (C) 10 days
- (D) 3 days **← answer**

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — In the half-year of appointment, half pay leave is credited at 5/3 days per completed calendar month.
Step 2 — Completed months are May and June — two.
Step 3 — 2 × 5/3 = 3.33, rounded to the nearest day under Rule 29(5) = 3 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — Fractions are rounded to the nearest day; leave is not carried in decimals.
- B — That applies the earned-leave rate of 2 1/2 days a month instead of the HPL rate of 5/3.
- C — That is the full half-yearly instalment of 10 days, which a mid-half-year entrant does not get.

**Exam-safe point: HPL pro-rata is 5/3 a month, not 2 1/2.**

> *Authority:* Rule 29(2)(a) and 29(5), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q14. A servant joins on 1 March. How much half pay leave is credited in that half-year?

- (A) 10 days
- (B) 7 days **← answer**
- (C) 10 days — and no other figure
- (D) 6 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — Completed months March to June = four.
Step 2 — 4 × 5/3 = 6.67 days.
Step 3 — Rounded to the nearest day = 7 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — The full instalment applies only to a servant on the rolls from 1 January.
- C — Ten is the whole half-year's credit. The option repeats another option and cannot be the unique key.
- D — That truncates 6.67 instead of rounding it; Rule 29(5) rounds.

**Exam-safe point: 5/3 per month, then round.**

> *Authority:* Rule 29(2)(a) and 29(5), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q15. A servant is treated as dies non for 54 days. By how many days is his half pay leave credit reduced under Rule 29(2)(d)?

- (A) 3 days **← answer**
- (B) 54 days
- (C) 10 days
- (D) 6 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The reduction is one-eighteenth of the dies non period.
Step 2 — 54 ÷ 18 = 3.
Step 3 — 3 is below the 10-day cap, so the full 3 is applied.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- B — The whole period is not deducted; it is reduced by a fraction of it.
- C — That is the cap, applied even though the computed figure is smaller.
- D — That divides by 9, which is not the rule's divisor.

**Exam-safe point: Dies non: one-eighteenth, capped at 10 days.**

> *Authority:* Rule 29(2)(d), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q16. A servant is dies non for 216 days. By how many days is his half pay leave credit reduced?

- (A) 10 days **← answer**
- (B) 12 days
- (C) 21.6 days
- (D) 216 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — One-eighteenth of 216 = 12 days.
Step 2 — The reduction is capped at 10 days.
Step 3 — So the credit is reduced by 10, not 12.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- B — That is the uncapped arithmetic; the rule caps it at 10.
- C — That divides by 10, the earned-leave divisor, instead of 18.
- D — The whole period is never deducted.

**Exam-safe point: Compute first, then apply the cap — not the other way round.**

> *Authority:* Rule 29(2)(d), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q20. A servant eligible for departmental leave under Rule 49 completes 12 months of actual duty, and by 30 June has completed 15 months. How much half pay leave is he entitled to as at 30 June under Rule 29(3)?

- (A) 25 days
- (B) 0 days
- (C) 20 days **← answer**
- (D) 10 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — Rule 29(3) is a milestone, not a running rate: it gives 20 days of half pay leave on the COMPLETION of 12 months of actual duty.
Step 2 — The 12-month milestone was crossed in March; the further 3 months add nothing, because the rule sets no second smaller step.
Step 3 — So the entitlement as at 30 June is 20 days. This class of servant is outside the ordinary 10-day half-yearly credit in Rule 29(1), which is the whole reason sub-rule (3) exists.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That pro-rates the grant as though it were a rate per month; Rule 29(3) fixes a completed-year milestone.
- B — Rule 29(3) exists precisely to give this class of servant a credit.
- D — That is the ordinary half-yearly credit for staff under Rule 29(1), not the Rule 49 entitlement.

**Exam-safe point: Rule 49 staff: 20 days of HPL on completing 12 months of actual duty. No pro-rating.**

> *Authority:* Rule 29(3) read with Rule 49, CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972


## Commuted leave and leave not due

### Q21. A servant holding 80 days of half pay leave takes 10 days of commuted leave. How much is debited against his half pay leave account?

- (A) 20 days **← answer**
- (B) 10 days
- (C) 30 days
- (D) 40 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — Commuted leave is debited at twice the leave granted.
Step 2 — 10 × 2 = 20 days.
Step 3 — The account falls from 80 to 60; the servant was away only 10 days.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- B — That is the one-for-one debit of ordinary half pay leave, not commuted leave.
- C — That adds the leave to the double debit instead of applying it.
- D — That doubles twice over.

**Exam-safe point: Commuted: two debited for one granted.**

> *Authority:* Rule 30(1)(d), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q22. A servant holds 50 days of half pay leave and needs 25 days away for surgery. He takes commuted leave. What remains in his half pay leave account?

- (A) 75 days
- (B) 0 days **← answer**
- (C) 25 days
- (D) -25 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — 25 days of commuted leave debit 25 × 2 = 50 days.
Step 2 — The account holds exactly 50.
Step 3 — So it is exhausted: nil remains, and he was on full pay throughout.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That adds rather than debits.
- C — That assumes a one-for-one debit, which is plain half pay leave, not commuted leave.
- D — Leave not due can create a negative HPL balance; commuted leave within the balance cannot.

**Exam-safe point: Full pay is bought with the double debit; the account pays for the pay.**

> *Authority:* Rule 30(1)(d) and Rule 40(4), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q23. A servant has already commuted 120 days of half pay leave for study during his service. He now seeks a further 90 days for an approved course. How much may be commuted without a medical certificate?

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The study route under Rule 30(1-A) allows up to 180 days in the entire service.
Step 2 — 120 days are already used, leaving 180 − 120 = 60.
Step 3 — So 60 days may be commuted; the other 30 would need the ordinary route.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

- B — That is the request, which would take the total to 210 against a 180-day lifetime limit.
- C — The limit is for the whole service, not for each application.

**Exam-safe point: Rule 30(1-A) is a lifetime limit of 180 days, not a per-application limit.**

> *Authority:* Rule 30(1-A), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q24. A servant drew 20 days of commuted leave at full pay and then resigned without rejoining duty. Pay plus DA was ₹72,000 a month. How much is recoverable under Rule 30(2)?

- (A) ₹12,000
- (B) ₹0
- (C) ₹48,000
- (D) ₹24,000 **← answer**

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The commuted leave is re-treated as half pay leave.
Step 2 — Full-pay leave salary for 20 days = 72,000 × 20/30 = ₹48,000.
Step 3 — Half-pay salary for the same 20 days = (72,000 ÷ 2) × 20/30 = ₹24,000. Difference = ₹24,000.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That halves the difference twice.
- B — No recovery is made only where the retirement is on ill-health grounds or on death; a resignation is not excused.
- C — That recovers the whole leave salary, leaving him unpaid for a period he did serve.

**Exam-safe point: Recover the difference between full pay and half pay, not the whole salary.**

> *Authority:* Rule 30(2) and its proviso, CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

> *Authority:* Rule 31(1), CCS (Leave) Rules, 1972

### Q26. A servant overstays his sanctioned leave by 35 days. He holds 20 days of half pay leave. How is the absence regularised?

## Encashment and the 300-day pot

### Q28. A servant retires with 200 days of earned leave and 180 days of half pay leave at credit. How many days are encashed?

- (A) 380 days
- (B) 200 days
- (C) 300 days **← answer**
- (D) 180 days

**Stepwise solution**

Step 1 — The combined encashment ceiling is 300 days.
Step 2 — Earned leave is encashed first and in full: 200 days.
Step 3 — The remaining ceiling is 100, so 100 of the 180 HPL days are encashed — 300 in all.
That is the whole calculation; nothing else in the rule moves the answer.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- A — That encashes the whole balance and ignores the 300-day ceiling.
- B — That encashes earned leave only; half pay leave is encashable on retirement.
- D — That is the half pay leave alone, and it is not the encashable total.
