# Batch 2: verification of examples, drills and underlying GID conditions

**Review date:** 28 October 2026. **Scope:** the supplied Batch 2 compiler and the four detailed video parts already produced, concerning CCS (LTC) Rules 7–11 and related Rule 7 instructions. This is a source-based review, not a determination of an individual claim. **Full verification of every GID and later amendment is not complete.** Historical reproductions establish what an earlier decision said; they do not by themselves prove that it remains unchanged. The 2025 guidelines state that, if their consolidation is imprecise or contradictory, the relevant OM/order prevails.

1. [DoPT guidelines of 3 September 2025, circulated by the Department of Telecommunications](https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/04/f9957f728fe3ee1e44dc0f8887f7d40f.pdf) — Rules 7–11 and the 17 notes under Rule 7. The DoT forwarding letter is dated **9 September 2025**, not 2026.

## A. Examples in the supplied compiler

| Example | Review | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Outward journey starts 28 December 2027; return 4 January 2028. | **Supported** by Rule 9: the concession counts against the block within which the outward journey commenced, even when the return falls later. | If an eligible regular hometown concession is being used against 2026–27, the outward date falls in that sub-block. Identify the actual concession and block used; carry-forward cases also follow Rule 9. |
| Eligible 2026–27 hometown LTC unused; use it in 2028 alongside an eligible 2028–29 LTC. | **Pattern supported**, subject to conditions. Rule 10 allows carry-over into the first year of the next block; FAQ Q16 expressly gives the analogous 2026 example. | The example assumes the employee is eligible, the first concession is carry-forward eligible, and both entitlements remain available. **Do not apply this normal-block illustration automatically to the fresh-recruit annual cycle.** |
| Posting and declared hometown both Pune; use any-place LTC instead. | **Supported** in the ordinary case by FAQ Q14 and Q19: no hometown LTC when headquarters/place of posting and hometown are the same; one any-place LTC per four-year block. | Actual eligibility, choice, block and available concession must still be verified. Fresh-recruit FAQ Q70 gives a separate treatment. |
| Employee joins 15 March 2026; spouse travels 20 December 2026; one-year gate not met. | **Supported on the stated facts** for the categories to which the continuous-service condition applies: under one year has elapsed on the spouse's journey date. | Service must actually be continuous. Rule 7(1) explicitly names Rule 1(3)(i) and (iii); Rule 2 separately addresses other specified categories and certification. The companion example of 20 March 2027 can meet the one-year gate if service is continuous, but that alone does not prove full claim eligibility. |

**Block-year date:** FAQ Q13, Table 1 expressly labels 2026–29 the next block after 2022–25; calling 2026–29 the running block on 28 October 2026 is a calendar-date application, not the text of a separate 2026 notification. The 2025 FAQ directly provides Q16 and Q17 examples in 2026.

**Rule 8(b) plus Rule 10:** The proviso adjusts any-place LTC against the hometown LTC available at commencement; Rule 10 links certain any-place carry-forward to carrying forward the second two-year hometown entitlement. The reviewed sources did not yield a worked example resolving every combined case. Do not give a categorical answer to a complex combination without the relevant original orders and written office clarification.

## B. Verification of the compiler's eight MCQs and flashcards

| Item | Source-based answer | Review |
|---|---|---|
| MCQ 1: Rule 7 one-year test date | Relevant employee's or family member's **journey date**. | Supported by Rule 7(1), subject to the categories named. |
| MCQ 2: leave | Any leave due and admissible, including casual, special casual and child-care leave. | Supported by Rule 7(2) and FAQ Q25. **Family-only travel is a separate exception to the need for the employee to take leave** (Q26); weekend/holidays alone for employee travel are insufficient (Q27). |
| MCQ 3: normal hometown LTC block | Once in a block of **two calendar years**. | Supported by Rule 8(a), subject to applicable special rules. |
| MCQ 4: family lives away at hometown | Employee may choose self-only annual hometown LTC **in lieu of all** otherwise admissible scheme concessions, including any-place LTC for self and family. | Supported by Rule 8(c). Do not describe it as an extra benefit in addition to normal family concessions. |
| MCQ 5: block on 28 October 2026 | **2026–29**, from the FAQ's table and calendar. | Supported as a date-based application; not a quotation of a separate 2026 order. |
| MCQ 6: December outward / January return | **Outward-journey block**. | Supported by Rule 9. |
| MCQ 7: headquarters and hometown same | Normally **one any-place LTC in the four-year block**, not hometown LTC. | Supported by FAQ Q14/Q19; special fresh-recruit treatment must be checked. |
| MCQ 8: normal carry-forward window | **First year of the next relevant block**. | Supported by Rule 10, with its second-sentence condition where applicable; fresh-recruit annual entitlement is not automatically covered. |
| Flashcard: Rule 7(1) named categories | Rule 1(3) clauses **(i) and (iii)**. | Supported; do not imply the one-year requirement never applies to other categories: see Rule 2. |
| Flashcard: technical resignation | Technical resignation within **eight years of appointment**, and eligible continuation after joining another government post. | The 2016 OM states the fresh-recruit period remains eight years from **initial appointment**. Verify that the resignation qualifies as technical; ordinary resignation is not automatically equivalent. |
| Flashcard: Rule 8(b) proviso | Any-place LTC in lieu of and adjusted against hometown LTC **available at commencement of journey**. | Supported; that time qualifier should not be omitted. |
| Flashcard: NCR | Delhi headquarters and a qualifying hometown outside Delhi in another NCR state: FAQ Q20 says such cities are not treated as Delhi headquarters. | Supported as the FAQ's rule, subject to declared hometown and other conditions. |
| Flashcard: Rule 11 | Each eligible member may choose a different place in the four-year block. | Supported; declaration and all other conditions remain applicable. |

**Correction to over-broad phrasing:** The compiler's narration says two LTCs are possible “only” in the first year of a sub-block. FAQ Q16 supports the listed carry-over-plus-current pattern at the beginning of sub-blocks; it does **not** by itself establish that every conceivable two-journey situation outside that pattern is impossible. Avoid the unqualified “only”.

The consolidation cites OM 43/1/55-Estt.(A) Part II, 11 October 1956. | The current travel entitlement and proof requirements may also be affected by later instructions. |
| 2–3 | No employee LTC on weekend/holiday alone without leave; CCL and study leave included. The consolidation cites 3 April 2018 and 30 March 1988 OMs; FAQ Q25–27 gives the leave/family distinction. | Verify study-leave journey/fare mechanics against the original 1988 OM and later instructions, rather than importing a general fare formula. |
| 4 | The 2016 technical-resignation OM, para 2.3, permits LTC entitlement to be carried forward where applicable; a person who technically resigns within eight years and joins another government post is treated as a fresh recruit for eight years from initial appointment. Its example: four years already served, four remaining. Para 2.1 explains proper-channel and other conditions. | Later amendments and actual acceptance/eligibility require checking; the video currently names the topic but does not teach all the underlying conditions. |
| 5 | The 2025 consolidation says a hometown declared outside India, subject to controlling officer's satisfaction, can attract LTC with limited government assistance. | Original order and calculation/conditions not independently verified. No amount should be asserted. |
| 6 | Historical official decisions assess family entitlement for outward and return independently. Historical illustrations include a dependent child obtaining employment/marrying after outward travel; no planned return with written relinquishment; a newly married spouse or legally adopted child in a return-only case. The 2025 consolidation cites OM 43/10/58-Ests.(A), 11 December 1958. | Historical age-fare examples and present-day application must be checked against later rules; they must not be represented as automatically current. |
| 7 | 2025 note recognizes India-based Mission/Post staff coming to India on home leave or home leave-cum-transfer. | Full underlying categories, journey legs and reimbursement conditions remain to be verified. |
| 8 | The 2025 consolidation distinguishes LTC combined with transfer and with tour; it cites OMs dated 19 July 1960 and 7 February 2007. | Detailed directions, route/fare caps and subsequent changes need original-order verification. Do not assume unrestricted reimbursement. |
| 9 | The 2025 summary contains an 'automatically becomes' training-HQ statement and a sentence referring to change by competent authority under SR 59. An older official compilation additionally says HQ normally continues unless changed under SR 59 and describes distinct India/abroad cases. | **Potentially inconsistent/ambiguous summary:** do not assert training always changes HQ. Check the underlying decision, SR 59, subsequent orders and the actual HQ declaration. |
| 10–11 | The 2025 consolidation describes an option between PSU and Central LTC and a fresh option when PSU rules change; for a top-level PSU post without LTC it notes a foreign-service-terms condition. The historical decision warns against use of both schemes for the same block. | Verify which scheme applies, deputation terms, block and revisions; do not assume both benefits can be combined. |
| 12 | 2025 note specifies Indian Aid Mission/Nepal programme staff: the relevant Indian end/start is the nearest Indian railway station to posting **from which onward travel actually starts or return ends**. | Full details and current application of the underlying order still need verification. |
| 13 | FAQ Q64–70 describes eight occasions, three hometown and fourth any-place in each four-occasion sequence; first two four-year blocks personal to fresh recruits; Q67 says annual fresh-recruit LTC is not carried to next year. Q66 also discusses choosing normal LTC and restrictions in that block. | Transition back to normal blocks is case-specific (Q68); the Part 2 video is only a summary. Check no-carry-over and personal-block exceptions when preparing examples. |
| 14 | The 2025 consolidation identifies specified regions, family left at old HQ/selected residence and no family transfer TA as conditions for options, and notes two additional emergency-passage occasions over a career. It cites OMs of 24 April 2018 and 28 February 2020. | Original OMs, eligibility, emergency definitions and later changes have not been fully checked. The supplied compiler associates 26 September 2014 with the next note: that citation is for fresh-recruit FAQs, not the 2018 regional-options OM. |
| 15 | DoPT's historical reproduction of OM 31011/8/89-Est.(A), 8 May 1990, permits specified government-employed spouses to choose separate hometowns and make separate claims; if one travels as the other's family member, that spouse cannot independently claim self for that block. Children may benefit as members of **one parent's family only** in a particular block. | Verify current instructions, employment status and exact claim facts. Part 2 mentions the no-double-self condition but does not narrate the children's one-parent condition. |
| 16 | The historical reproduction of OM 31011/4/91-Estt.(A), 9 July 1991, lists: prior HoD approval each occasion; disability requiring escort; no adult family member; use available rail/bus fare concessions; and no other LTC-entitled person accompanying. | The 2025 consolidation summarizes the facility as conditional, rather than reproducing those five conditions. Their present status and later disability-related amendments must be checked before treating the old conditions as exhaustive/current. |
| 17 | 2025 guidelines and FAQ Q12 state the suspended employee cannot personally take LTC, but eligible family may. | Check admissibility of each family member and later orders; do not infer that suspension gives the employee a fresh block. |

- The 2025 consolidation is a starting point, not a complete proof that every historical instruction is in force. The underlying orders and later changes for Notes 5–12, 14 and 16 remain materially incomplete; the original Gazette and full amendment chain are not established.
- A complete current inventory of all GIDs attached to Rules 7–11 has not been established. The available official historical compilation contains additional decisions whose currency must be assessed individually.
