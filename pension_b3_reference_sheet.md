## CHAPTER 1 • RULE 31: EMOLUMENTS
**RULE 31(1) — WHAT COUNTS AS EMOLUMENTS?**  (RULE 31(1))

Q: What exactly are emoluments, and which pay figure and which date do we use?

### THE RULE • RULE 31(1)
Rule 31(1): 'emoluments' means basic pay as defined in Fundamental Rule 9(21)(a)(i), which a Government servant was drawing immediately before his retirement or on the date of his death, and includes non-practising allowance granted to a medical officer. The word is 'means', not 'includes', so the list is exhaustive. [Verbatim Gazette text, Rule 31(1) and its Explanation (G.S.R. 868(E) of 20.12.2021).]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Chaliye Rule thirty-one se shuru karein. Emoluments matlab wahi basic pay jo retirement se thik pehle, ya death ki date par mil rahi thi, plus medical officer ka non-practising allowance. Ye allowance aise doctor ko milta hai jo private practice nahi kar sakta.

### THE EXPLANATION
Explanation to Rule 31(1): stagnation increment is treated as emoluments for the calculation of retirement benefits. A stagnation increment is the extra increment given to someone who has reached the top of the pay scale and cannot go higher. [Verbatim Gazette text.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Ab Explanation. Stagnation increment bhi emoluments me ginega. Ye wo extra increment hai jo pay scale ke top par pahunchne ke baad milta hai, aage badhne ki jagah nahi bachti. To retirement benefits me ise zaroor jodna hai.

### REAL EXAMPLE
Example (invented names and amounts, recomputed): Charu's basic pay just before retirement is Rs 62,000; her dearness allowance, house rent allowance and transport allowance are NOT added, so her emoluments are Rs 62,000. Dr Dev, a medical officer, has basic pay Rs 1,00,000 and non-practising allowance Rs 12,000, so his emoluments are 1,00,000 + 12,000 = Rs 1,12,000. Time test: the pay drawn immediately before retirement, or on the date of death. Q&A from the source: is a medical officer's non-practising allowance emoluments? Yes, Rule 31(1) says so expressly.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Example dekhiye. Charu ki basic pay sixty-two hazaar hai, to emoluments sixty-two hazaar, koi allowance nahi judta. Dr Dev: basic one lakh plus NPA twelve hazaar, total one lakh twelve hazaar.

### THE TRAP
DA, HRA, TA and LTC are not emoluments: only basic pay (plus NPA for a medical officer). The source guide's worked-example annotation 'emoluments = basic pay + DA' conflicts with Rule 31(1), which has no DA; do not use it (divergence confirmed against the rule text). Look-alike: Rule 31 DEFINES emoluments; Rule 32 AVERAGES them over ten months. Do not mix the two.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Yahan trap hai. Rule me shabd means likha hai, matlab list poori hai. DA, HRA, TA emoluments nahi hain. Kisi note me basic plus DA likha ho to galat. Aur yaad rakhiye, Rule thirty-one define karta hai, Rule thirty-two average nikalta hai.

> REMEMBER: Emoluments = basic pay (+ NPA for doctors) just before retirement or death. No DA!

## CHAPTER 2 • RULE 31(2) TO (11): SPECIAL CASES
**RULE 31(2)-(11) — WHEN THE PAY ACTUALLY DRAWN IS NOT THE WHOLE STORY**  (RULE 31(2)-(11))

Q: What do sub-rules 2 to 11 of Rule 31 add, for leave, suspension, deputation and late pay revision?

### SUB-RULE (2): LEAVE, SUSPENSION
Rule 31(2) (resolved from a rule summary): the pay he would have drawn during leave with salary, extraordinary leave on medical certificate, or suspension that ended in reinstatement is counted. Increases not actually drawn are excluded, except the increments or notional increases covered by sub-rules (5), (10) and (11). The source guide called this a 'notional increments trigger'; that description is not right (status: changed). Second-hand summary: read the Gazette for the exact words.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Sub-rule two: paid leave, medical certificate wali extraordinary leave, ya suspension ke baad reinstatement. In sab me jo pay milta rehta tha wo count hoga. Jo increase actually nahi mila wo chhut jayega, sirf sub-rules five, ten aur eleven chhodkar.

### SUB-RULES (3) TO (9)
Rule 31(3): where the servant, immediately before retirement or death while in service, had proceeded on leave with leave salary after holding a higher appointment (officiating or temporary), the higher-post emoluments count ONLY if it is certified that he would have continued to hold that higher appointment but for proceeding on leave. Rule 31(4): where the absence was extraordinary leave or suspension that does not count as service, the emoluments drawn immediately before proceeding on leave or being placed under suspension count. Rule 31(5): an increment earned during leave, not withheld, counts though not drawn. Rule 31(6): pay drawn on deputation to an ex-cadre post in the same or another Department OR to the Armed Forces of India is emoluments; proviso: where he is on leave after release from the ex-cadre post on completion of the deputation period, the pay he would have drawn in the parent department counts. Rule 31(7): pay on deputation to a State Government or on foreign service does NOT count; only the Central pay he would have drawn counts. Rule 31(8): a re-employed pensioner who elected to retain his earlier pension and whose re-employment pay was reduced by an amount not exceeding that pension, the element of pension by which pay was reduced is emoluments. Rule 31(9): a servant transferred to a PSU or autonomous body on conversion who OPTS to retain Government pensionary benefits has the PSU or body pay treated as emoluments. [Verbatim Gazette text, Rules 31(3) to 31(9).]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Baaki sub-rules ek line me. Teen: leave ke dauraan higher post. Chaar: non-qualifying leave ya suspension se pehle ki pay. Paanch: leave me earned increment. Chhah: ex-cadre post ya Armed Forces. Saat: State ya foreign service me Central pay. Aath: re-employed pensioner. Nau: converted PSU ka employee.

### SUB-RULES (10) AND (11)
Rule 31(10): notional pay from a retrospective revision after retirement (retrospective scale increase, or retrospective promotion by a review DPC, exoneration or court order). Rule 31(11): death during a penalty that reduced pay: notional pay ignoring the penalty. Example (invented, recomputed): Bilal retires on 31 January 2026 on Rs 80,000; a review Departmental Promotion Committee later promotes him from 1 October 2025 to a post paying Rs 84,000 from that date; his notional emoluments are Rs 84,000.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Sub-rule das ka example. Bilal thirty-first January ko eighty hazaar par retire hua. Baad me review DPC ne first October se eighty-four hazaar deni thi, to emoluments eighty-four hazaar maane jayenge. Gyarah: death ke waqt penalty chal rahi thi, to penalty ke bina wali pay.

### THE TRAP
Gap in the source guide: Rule 31(3)-(11) were never mentioned and are added here; the guide's 'notional increments trigger' label for Rule 31(2) is wrong. Cross-link: sub-rule (10) and (11) feed Rule 32(5) and (6) for the average. [Verbatim Gazette text, Rules 31(10), 31(11), 32(5) and 32(6).]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap ye hai. Guide ne sub-rules teen se gyarah chhod diye the. Aur sub-rule two ko notional increments trigger kehna galat hai, wo leave aur suspension ka rule hai. Exact words Gazette se confirm kar lein.

> REMEMBER: Rule 31 has 11 sub-rules; (2) is about leave and suspension, (10)-(11) are about notional pay!

## CHAPTER 3 • RULE 32(1): AVERAGE EMOLUMENTS
**RULE 32(1) — AVERAGE EMOLUMENTS OF THE LAST TEN MONTHS**  (RULE 32(1))

Q: What are average emoluments, and when do we use them instead of plain emoluments?

### THE RULE • RULE 32(1)
Rule 32(1): average emoluments shall be determined with reference to the emoluments drawn by a Government servant during the last ten months of his service. The base is the Rule 31 emoluments only. Rule 32 has no Explanation, only an Illustration (confirmed). [Verbatim Gazette text, Rules 32(1) to 32(6).]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Rule thirty-two, one: average emoluments matlab service ke aakhri das mahino ki emoluments, jodkar das se divide. Pay wahi Rule thirty-one wali, matlab basic plus doctor ka NPA, DA nahi.

### WHICH ONE WINS? RULE 44(1)
Rule 44(1): pension at 50% of emoluments or average emoluments, whichever is more beneficial to the Government servant, subject to a minimum of Rs 9,000 and a maximum of Rs 1,25,000 per month. Dynamic figures: effective dates not re-confirmed here; check the latest O.M. Full treatment of Rule 44 is in the next batch. Average emoluments are an option, not always the best.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Ab choice. Rule forty-four, one: pension fifty percent emoluments ya average emoluments ka, jo zyada faydemand ho. Minimum nau hazaar, maximum one lakh twenty-five hazaar per month, par latest O.M. se confirm kar lein.

### ROUNDING • RULE 44(9)
Rule 44(9): amounts are expressed in whole rupees and any fraction is rounded up to the next higher rupee (confirmed, close restatement). The source guide cited this as the rounding rule for Rule 32 averages. Example: 77,222.22 becomes 77,223.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Chhoti baat, par exam me poochi jaati hai. Rule forty-four, nine: rakam poore rupaye me, aur fraction upar round hota hai. To seventy-seven thousand two hundred twenty-two point twenty-two banega seventy-seven thousand two hundred twenty-three.

### THE TRAP
The source said Rule 32(2) means 'if pay fell in the last ten months, average is computed as if no reduction'. That is a wrong attribution (status: changed). Rule 32(2) deals with the pay he would have drawn during leave with salary, extraordinary leave on medical certificate, or suspension ending in reinstatement. Ignoring a penalty reduction is Rule 32(6), only where the officer dies while a penalty-reduced pay was running. An ordinary pay fall counts as actually drawn; the average can still win through the Rule 44(1) choice, not through Rule 32(2). Do not teach the source version.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: Rule thirty-two, two ye nahi kehta ki pay girne par purani pay maan lo. Wo leave aur suspension ka rule hai. Pay girna normal tarike se count hoga, average sirf isliye jeetta hai ki Rule forty-four better figure chunne deta hai.

> REMEMBER: Average = last 10 months; pension uses the BETTER of emoluments or average; fractions go UP!

## CHAPTER 4 • RULE 32(2) TO (6): THE TEN-MONTH WINDOW
**RULE 32(2)-(6) — LEAVE, SUSPENSION AND THE 30-DAY MONTH**  (RULE 32(2)-(6))

Q: How is the ten-month window handled when there is leave, suspension or a late pay revision?

### SUB-RULE (2) AND ITS PROVISO
Rule 32(2): pay he would have drawn during leave with salary, extraordinary leave on medical certificate, or suspension ending in reinstatement is included. The PROVISO belongs to sub-rule (2), not (3): increases not actually drawn are excluded, except those in sub-rules (4), (5) and (6). [Verbatim Gazette text.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Pehle sub-rule two. Paid leave, medical extraordinary leave, ya reinstatement wali suspension, in me jo pay milta wo count hota hai. Proviso: jo increase actually nahi mila wo nahi jodenge, sirf sub-rules four, five aur six ke cases me.

### SUB-RULE (3): THE WINDOW SLIDES
Rule 32(3): periods of extraordinary leave or suspension that do not count as service are disregarded, and an equal period earlier is added in, so the window is still ten months and the divisor stays ten. For fractions of a month, a month is taken as 30 days. Illustration: retirement on 16 July 2019 gives nine full months (October 2018 to June 2019) plus 14/30 of September 2018 plus 16/30 of July 2019, which together make ten months.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Ab sub-rule three, jise log galat karte hain. Jo EOL ya suspension service me nahi ginta, wo mahine hata do, aur barabar ke pehle ke mahine jod do. Window das mahine hi rehti hai, divisor das hi. Ek mahina thirty din. Sixteenth July two thousand nineteen par retirement, to nau poore mahine, September ke fourteen-thirtieth aur July ke sixteen-thirtieth.

### SUB-RULES (4), (5), (6)
Rule 32(4): an increment earned during leave in the last ten months, not withheld, is included though not drawn. Rule 32(5): retrospective notional pay increases within the last ten months, in the same circumstances as Rule 31(10), are included. Rule 32(6): where a penalty reduced pay and the officer dies during it, notional pay ignoring the penalty is used for the last ten months. These match Rule 31(5), (10), (11).

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Sub-rules four, five, six exceptions hain. Chaar: leave me earned increment, jo rokha na gaya ho. Paanch: backdated notional pay increase. Chhah: penalty ke dauraan death ho gayi to penalty ke bina wali pay.

### THE TRAP
Source errors corrected: (a) the source said the excluded months are dropped and the average is taken over the remaining months, so the denominator shrinks; that is wrong (status: changed). (b) The source's mismatch over sub-rule (4) (bullet said increment, bare text said extraordinary leave or suspension exclusion) is resolved: (4) is the increment; the exclusion is in (3). (c) Source examples (promotion benefit from outside the window; arrears of 'Chhath Pay Commission', a slip for Sixth or Seventh CPC) are the source's own, not in the rule; arrears of a retrospective revision after retirement may still count through Rule 32(5) and Rule 31(10). Those two source examples remain unverified.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: purane notes kehte hain ki hataye gaye mahine divisor ghata dete hain, to nau ya aath se divide karo. Galat. Window peeche khiskti hai aur divisor das hi rehta hai. Aur exclusion sub-rule three me hai, four me nahi.

> REMEMBER: Skip bad months, add earlier ones, still divide by TEN; a month = 30 days!

## CHAPTER 5 • RULE 32: WORKED EXAMPLE WITH LEAVE
**RULE 32 — A FULL WORKED EXAMPLE WITH EXTRAORDINARY LEAVE**  (RULE 32 • EXAMPLE)

Q: Can you walk me through a complete average-emoluments sum when part of the time was extraordinary leave?

### THE FACTS (INVENTED)
Example (invented, flagged as the card writer's own numbers): Bilal retires on 31 March 2027. December to March: 4 months at Rs 80,000. September to November: 3 months of extraordinary leave that does not count as qualifying service. Before that, March to August: pay Rs 75,000. Why rebuilt: the source's own Example 9 (Ashok Kumar, 75,000 for 5 months, 3 months leave, 80,000 for 4 months, dividing by 9) uses the wrong method and its 12 months from April do not fit a ten-month window.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Worked example, naam aur numbers apne. Bilal thirty-first March two thousand twenty-seven ko retire hota hai. Aakhri chaar mahine, December se March, eighty hazaar. September se November EOL, jo service me nahi ginti. Us se pehle March se August, seventy-five hazaar.

### STEP 1 AND 2: BUILD THE WINDOW
Under Rule 32(3): window = Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar (4 months) + the three leave months skipped + Aug, Jul, Jun, May, Apr, Mar of the earlier year (6 months) = 10 months. Total = 4 x 80,000 + 6 x 75,000 = 3,20,000 + 4,50,000 = Rs 7,70,000. Divisor stays ten.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Pehle window banao. Chaar mahine eighty hazaar ke. Teen leave mahine hata do. Phir pehle ke chhah mahine seventy-five hazaar ke jod do, kul das mahine. Total: chaar into eighty hazaar, three lakh twenty hazaar, plus chhah into seventy-five hazaar, four lakh fifty hazaar, seven lakh seventy hazaar.

### STEP 3 TO 5: COMPARE
Average emoluments = 7,70,000 / 10 = Rs 77,000 (no fraction, so no rounding). Emoluments at retirement = Rs 80,000. Under Rule 44(1) take the more beneficial: 80,000. Pension at 50% = Rs 40,000 per month; with the average it would be 50% of 77,000 = Rs 38,500. The 50 percent rate is the Rule 44(1) rate as confirmed here; qualifying-service scaling is in the next batch, so assume full service for this example.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Ab comparison. Seven lakh seventy hazaar divided by ten, seventy-seven hazaar, yahi average. Actual emoluments eighty hazaar, jo zyada hai. To Rule forty-four, one se eighty hazaar lenge, pension fifty percent, forty hazaar. Average se sirf thirty-eight hazaar five hundred milta.

### THE TRAP
Source's method (wrong): 9 qualifying months, total 6,95,000 divided by 9 = 77,222.22, rounded up to 77,223, wrong-average pension 38,611.5. Arithmetic matched the source, but the method is wrong under Rule 32(3). Rounding correction: 38,611.5 rounds UP to Rs 38,612 under Rule 44(9), not 38,611.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: guide ne leave mahine hata kar nau se divide kiya. Sahi tarika window khiskana aur das se divide karna hai. Aur thirty-eight thousand six hundred eleven point five rupaye upar round hokar thirty-eight thousand six hundred twelve banega.

> REMEMBER: Build ten months, divide by ten, compare with actual pay, take the better; round UP!

## CHAPTER 6 • RULE 32: PROMOTION CASES AND WHEN THE AVERAGE WINS
**RULE 32 — FOUR PROMOTION CASES, AND THE ONE TIME AVERAGE WINS**  (RULE 32 • CASES)

Q: When pay changes in the last ten months, which is better, emoluments or the average?

### CASES 1 AND 2
Case 1 (early promotion): 3 x 60,000 = 1,80,000; 7 x 85,000 = 5,95,000; total 7,75,000; /10 = 77,500; actual 85,000 is better; 50% = Rs 42,500. Case 2 (mid promotion): 5 x 65,000 = 3,25,000; 5 x 90,000 = 4,50,000; total 7,75,000; average 77,500; actual 90,000; 50% = Rs 45,000. Source's four cases are its own illustrations; arithmetic recomputed here.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Chaar log, jinka promotion aakhri das mahino me hai. Case one: teen mahine sixty hazaar, saat mahine eighty-five hazaar. Average seventy-seven hazaar five hundred, actual eighty-five hazaar, pension forty-two hazaar five hundred. Case two: paanch aur paanch, sixty-five aur ninety hazaar, average phir seventy-seven hazaar five hundred, actual ninety hazaar, pension forty-five hazaar.

### CASES 3 AND 4
Case 3 (late promotion): 8 x 70,000 = 5,60,000; 2 x 95,000 = 1,90,000; total 7,50,000; average 75,000; actual 95,000; 50% = Rs 47,500; gap between actual and average = Rs 20,000. Case 4 (no promotion): 10 x 78,000; average and actual both 78,000; 50% = Rs 39,000.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Case three: aath mahine seventy hazaar, do mahine ninety-five hazaar. Average seventy-five hazaar, actual ninety-five hazaar, pension forty-seven hazaar five hundred. Case four: promotion nahi, dono seventy-eight hazaar, pension thirty-nine hazaar.

### WHEN THE AVERAGE WINS
Source Q&A: 5 x 90,000 = 4,50,000; 5 x 70,000 = 3,50,000; total 8,00,000; /10 = 80,000. Actual at retirement 70,000. Average is better: 50% = Rs 40,000 against Rs 35,000. Principle holds through the Rule 44(1) 'whichever is more beneficial' choice, not through Rule 32(2). Takeaway: promotion in the window, emoluments win; no change, both equal; pay fall, the average wins.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Ab wo ek case jahan average jeetta hai, jab pay giri ho. Paanch mahine ninety hazaar, phir paanch mahine seventy hazaar. Average eighty hazaar, actual seventy hazaar. Pension average par forty hazaar, actual par thirty-five hazaar. Ye Rule forty-four ke better-of choice se hota hai, Rule thirty-two, two se nahi.

### THE TRAP
Divergence confirmed: the guide table shows 78,250 for Case 1; recomputing from its own numbers gives 77,500. Final results of all four cases do not change because actual emoluments are higher in each. Case 2 average 77,500, Case 3 75,000 and Case 4 78,000 match the guide. These are illustrative figures; the principle is permanent, the numbers come from the current O.M.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: guide table me case one ka average seventy-eight hazaar two hundred fifty tha. Sahi seventy-seven hazaar five hundred hai. Pension same rehti hai, par sum khud karna sikhiye.

> REMEMBER: Promotion in the window, actual pay wins; no change, equal; pay fell, average wins!

## CHAPTER 7 • RULES 33 AND 34(1): RETIRING ON TIME OR EARLY
**RULES 33 AND 34(1) — SUPERANNUATION PENSION AND RETIRING PENSION**  (RULES 33 • 34(1))

Q: What is superannuation pension, and what are the three routes to retiring pension?

### RULE 33: SUPERANNUATION
Rule 33: superannuation pension or superannuation service gratuity, as the case may be, shall be granted under Rule 44 to a Government servant who retires on attaining the age of superannuation or, if his service was extended beyond that age, on the expiry of the extension. Two triggers: reaching the age, or the end of the extension. 'Superannuation' simply means retirement on reaching the fixed retiring age. [Verbatim Gazette text.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Pehle Rule thirty-three. Superannuation matlab fixed retiring age par retirement. Rule thirty-three superannuation pension ya superannuation service gratuity deta hai, Rule forty-four ke hisaab se. Do triggers: age aa gayi, ya extension khatam ho gayi.

### RULE 34(1): THREE ROUTES
Rule 34(1): retiring pension (or service gratuity) is granted to a Government servant who retires before superannuation (a) voluntarily, under Rule 43 or Fundamental Rule 56; (b) under the Special Voluntary Retirement Scheme, on being declared surplus (DoPT O.M. No. 25013/6/2001-Estt.(A) dated 28.02.2002, as amended from time to time); or (c) when retired by the Government under Rule 42 or FR 56. Rules 42 and 43 are covered in the next batch. [Verbatim Gazette text.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Ab Rule thirty-four, one. Retiring pension un logon ke liye jo age se pehle nikalte hain, teen raaste. Clause a: khud retire karein, Rule forty-three ya Fundamental Rule fifty-six. Clause b: surplus declare hokar Special VRS lein. Clause c: Government retire kare, Rule forty-two ya FR fifty-six.

### REAL EXAMPLE
Example (invented): Asha retires on reaching the age of superannuation, so Rule 33 applies. Dev applies to retire before that age under Rule 43, so Rule 34(1)(a). A clerk Esha is declared surplus and opts for the Special VRS, so Rule 34(1)(b). Farid is retired by the Government in the public interest under Rule 42, so Rule 34(1)(c).

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Example: Asha age par retire hoti hai, Rule thirty-three. Dev Rule forty-three se jaldi nikalta hai, clause a. Esha surplus hokar Special VRS leti hai, clause b. Farid ko Government retire karta hai Rule forty-two se, clause c.

### THE TRAP
Look-alike: (a) is the employee's choice (Rule 43 or FR 56); (c) is the Government's decision (Rule 42 or FR 56). The guide's claim that Rule 42 is a Government-initiated premature retirement after thirty years, with no ex-gratia like Rule 34(2), is UNVERIFIED here: Rule 42 was not read for this batch; verify it in Batch 4. All three routes lead to a retiring pension worked out under Rule 44.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: clause a aur clause c ek jaise lagte hain. Faisla kaun karta hai? Clause a me employee, clause c me Government. Rule forty-two ki detail agle batch me.

> REMEMBER: Rule 33 = retire on time. Rule 34(1) = early: you choose, Special VRS, or Government decides!

## CHAPTER 8 • RULE 34(2): THE SPECIAL VOLUNTARY RETIREMENT SCHEME
**RULE 34(2) — EX-GRATIA ON TOP OF PENSION UNDER THE SPECIAL VRS**  (RULE 34(2))

Q: Under the Special VRS, does the ex-gratia replace the pension, and who can use it?

### THE RULE • RULE 34(2)
Rule 34(2): a permanent Government servant declared surplus in his establishment, who opts for the Special Voluntary Retirement Scheme, is entitled to ex-gratia as per the Scheme in addition to the retiring pension or service gratuity under Rule 44 and the retirement gratuity under Rule 45. [Verbatim Gazette text.] Ex-gratia means a payment made as a favour or kindness, not as a legal right to a fixed sum.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Rule thirty-four, two: permanent servant, jo surplus declare hua ho, Special Voluntary Retirement Scheme chune to ex-gratia milta hai. Keyword hai in addition. Rule forty-four ki pension ya service gratuity, aur Rule forty-five ki retirement gratuity, dono ke upar.

### THE SCHEME O.M.
The O.M. number 25013/6/2001-Estt.(A) is confirmed from the text of Rule 34; the date 28.02.2002 is confirmed on the DoPT page. Scheme terms as reported there (NOT confirmed against the latest amended O.M.; add only after checking it): option within 3 months of being declared surplus; ex-gratia of 35 days pay plus DA for each completed year of service and 25 days for each year of service left, service capped at 33 years, minimum Rs 25,000 or 250 days; 5 years added to qualifying service for those with 15 or more years (1972 Rules wording).

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Scheme DoPT ke office memorandum number twenty-five thousand thirteen by six by two thousand one, Establishment A, twenty-eighth February two thousand two se aati hai, samay samay par amended. Payment ke figures latest amended O.M. se confirm karein.

### REAL EXAMPLE
Example (invented, no amounts because the Scheme figures are unconfirmed): Esha, a permanent clerk in an office that is being closed, is declared surplus. She opts for the Special VRS. She receives (1) retiring pension or service gratuity under Rule 44, (2) retirement gratuity under Rule 45 and (3) the ex-gratia under the Scheme. Surplus means the post itself has become excess; the Special VRS is a dignified route (source gloss, consistent with the Scheme).

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Example: Esha permanent clerk hai, uska post cut ho gaya, surplus declare hui. Special VRS liya, to retiring pension, retirement gratuity, aur ex-gratia, teeno mile.

### THE TRAP
Trap: the ex-gratia does not replace the Rule 44 and Rule 45 benefits; it is additional. Rule 34(2) needs both conditions: permanent servant and declared surplus. Three flavours of early exit: 34(1)(a) the employee chose, no surplus; 34(1)(b) the employee chose after being declared surplus (ex-gratia available); 34(1)(c) the Government chose. The guide's contrast with Rule 42 (Government's own initiative) is unverified here; see Batch 4.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: ex-gratia pension ka substitute nahi, addition hai. Aur do conditions saath chahiye, permanent bhi, surplus bhi. Teen early-exit flavours alag rakhein: employee ki choice, surplus ke baad choice, aur Government ki choice.

> REMEMBER: Special VRS = permanent + surplus. Ex-gratia is ADDED to pension and retirement gratuity!

## CHAPTER 9 • RULE 35(1)-(2): ABSORPTION IN A STATE GOVERNMENT
**RULE 35(1)-(2) — MOVING TO A STATE GOVERNMENT: DEEMED RETIREMENT**  (RULE 35(1)-(2))

Q: What happens to pension when a Central servant is absorbed in a State Government post?

### THE RULE • RULE 35(1)
Rule 35(1): a Government servant who is permitted to be absorbed in a State Government service or post shall be deemed to have retired from Central Government service from the date of absorption, and shall be eligible for pension or service gratuity and retirement gratuity on the qualifying service and emoluments as on that date, under Rules 44 and 45, subject to sub-rule (6). Absorption means being taken on permanently in the new employer's service. [Verbatim Gazette text, Rule 35(1) and its proviso.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Pehle rule. Absorption matlab naye employer dwara permanently liya jaana. State Government me absorption par wo Central service se deemed retired ho jata hai, usi date se. Deemed matlab maan liya gaya. Pension ya service gratuity, aur retirement gratuity, us date ki qualifying service aur emoluments par.

### THE PROVISO: A GRATUITY CAP
Proviso to Rule 35(1): the total gratuity from the Central and State services together shall not exceed the amount that would have been admissible had he continued in Central service and retired on the pay drawn at retirement from the State service. Example (invented): Hari's Central gratuity plus State gratuity add up to Rs 16,00,000, but the notional Central gratuity on his last State pay is Rs 14,00,000; only Rs 14,00,000 in total is allowed.

A penalty that permanently reduces pay (for example a reduction to a lower stage for a specified number of years with cumulative effect) does not fit this wording, so do not read 31(11) as a general 'ignore every penalty' rule. [Verbatim Gazette text; the reading of 'cumulative effect' penalties is the standard departmental one.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: sub-rule gyarah aur Rule thirty-two, chhah sirf us penalty par lagte hain jo pay sirf penalty ke dauraan ghatati hai aur khatam hone par pay wapas mil jati. Permanent ya cumulative effect wali reduction isme nahi aati, to ise har penalty par mat lagayein.

> REMEMBER: certificate for 31(3), Armed Forces plus proviso in 31(6), options in 31(8)-(9), penalty only while it runs in 31(11).

## CHAPTER 27 • RULE 32(3): THE FRACTION RULES
**RULE 32 — THE FRACTION RULES: 14/30, 16/30 AND FEBRUARY**  (RULE 32(3) • FRACTIONS)

Q: How exactly do you count a broken month, and what happens to February?

### 14/30 AND 16/30
Illustration to Rule 32(3), verbatim: a Government servant retires on 16th July 2019. The last ten months comprise nine full months and fractions of fourteen days of September 2018 and sixteen days of July 2019. The emoluments for the fractional periods are computed by multiplying the emolument by the factor 14/30 and 16/30 respectively, irrespective of the number of days in the month. Note the arithmetic: 9 + 14/30 + 16/30 = 10 exactly. The two broken ends at the start and the finish add up to one whole month.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Illustration rule me hi diya hai. Sixteenth July two thousand nineteen par retirement. Nau poore mahine, plus September two thousand eighteen ke fourteen-thirtieth, plus July ke sixteen-thirtieth. Total exactly das mahine. Factors din se bante hain, mahine ke actual dinon se nahi.

### FEBRUARY IS ALWAYS 30
The Illustration to Rule 32(3) ends with: 'This formula shall also apply in the case of the month of February, irrespective of whether the month has twenty eight days or twenty nine days.' So a February slice is never given a 28 or 29 denominator, and a leap year changes nothing. This is the single most common arithmetic slip in average-emoluments answers.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* February ka special case. Rule kehta hai chahe twenty-eight din ho ya twenty-nine, factor thirty hi rahega. Isliye February kabhi average ko tilt nahi karta, aur leap year se kuch nahi badalta.

### THE DIVISOR NEVER MOVES
Five situations and one divisor. (1) Ten clean months: divide by ten. (2) Extraordinary leave or suspension that does not count: the period is disregarded and an equal period earlier is added, still ten. (3) A broken month at either end: days/30, still ten. (4) February anywhere in the window: still /30, still ten. (5) An increment earned during leave and not withheld: included though not drawn, still ten. Rule 44(9) then rounds the final rupee amount up.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Table dekhiye. Paanch situations, divisor ek hi, das. Jo badalta hai wo window hai, divide karne wali sankhya nahi. Yahi is rule ka core hai, aur yahi sabse zyada galat hota hai.

### THE TRAP
Rule 32(3) treats a month as thirty days only 'in order that the fractions of a month, when added, worked out to one full month'. It is a fractions device, not a pay device. A whole month's emoluments are the emoluments actually drawn in that month, and a whole month is not converted to 30/30 of anything. Confusing the two produces a wrong average that still looks arithmetically neat.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: thirty din ka mahina ka matlab thirty din ki pay nahi. Ye factor sirf adhoore mahino ke liye hai, taaki fractions jodkar poora mahina ban jaye. Poore mahine me jo pay actually mili wahi ginegi.

> REMEMBER: fractions are days over 30, February is always 30, and the divisor never moves off ten.

## CHAPTER 28 • RULES 37(30)-(31) AND 38(26)-(29)
**THE CLAUSES NOBODY QUOTES: DISINVESTMENT AND THE PRASAR BHARATI CARVE-OUT**  (RULES 37(30) • 38(29))

Q: Which closing sub-rules of Rules 37 and 38 are never in a guide, and why do they matter?

### 37(30) AND 38(27) • THE 51 PER CENT TRIGGER
Rule 37(30): in case the Government disinvests its equity in any public sector undertaking to the extent of fifty-one per cent or more, it shall specify adequate safeguards for protecting the interest of the absorbed employees of such undertaking. Rule 38(27) carries the identical duty for an autonomous body. The duty is mandatory ('shall'), and it is triggered by the size of the disinvestment, not by a request from the employees. [Verbatim Gazette text.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Rule thirty-seven, thirty, aur Rule thirty-eight, twenty-seven. Government apni equity fifty-one percent ya usse zyada bech de, to absorbed employees ke liye adequate safeguards dena zaroori hai. Ye clause koi note nahi likhta, par duty mandatory hai.

### 37(31) AND 38(28) • WHAT A SAFEGUARD IS
Rule 37(31): the safeguards shall include option for voluntary retirement or continued service in the undertaking or retirement benefits on terms applicable to Government employees or employees of the PSU as per the option of the employee, and assured payment of earned pensionary benefits with relaxation in period of qualifying service, as may be decided by the Government. Rule 38(28) is the parallel provision for a body. So 'adequate safeguards' is not a blank cheque: the rule itself lists the menu.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Safeguards me kya hoga? Voluntary retirement ya service continue karne ka option, Government ya body ke terms par benefits jaisa employee chune, aur earned pensionary benefits ki assured payment, qualifying service me relaxation ke saath.

### 38(26) • PF, LEAVE, AND A LATER DISMISSAL
Rule 38(26)(a): the balance of provident fund credited to absorbed employees is, with the consent of the body, transferred to the new PF account. (b): earned leave and half pay leave stand transferred to the body. (c): dismissal or removal by the body after absorption for subsequent misconduct shall NOT amount to forfeiture of the retirement benefits for Government service, and a dismissal, removal or retrenchment decision is subject to review by the Ministry administratively concerned. Rule 37(29)(a)-(c) is the PSU twin, but with (c) rewritten by G.S.R. 340(E) of 22.05.2025 so that dismissal or removal by the PSU DOES forfeit. [Verbatim Gazette text, Rule 38(26); Rule 37(29)(c) as substituted.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Rule thirty-eight, twenty-six Rule thirty-seven, twenty-nine ka mirror hai, par ek bada farq. Rule thirty-eight me body ki dismissal se Government service ke benefits forfeit nahi hote, aur Ministry review karti hai. Rule thirty-seven me two thousand twenty-five ke baad wo forfeiture ho gayi.

### 38(29) • THE PRASAR BHARATI CARVE-OUT
Rule 38(29): nothing contained in Rule 38 shall be applicable to officers or employees including members of the Indian Information Service, Central Secretariat Service or any other service, or to persons borne on cadres outside Akashvani and Doordarshan, serving in Akashvani and Doordarshan and engaged in the performance of functions transferred to Prasar Bharati established under the Prasar Bharati (Broadcasting Corporation of India) Act, 1990. Rule 37 has no such general carve-out; its carve-out is the BSNL and MTNL one in Rules 37(26) to 37(28). [Verbatim Gazette text.]

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Sabse anokha exception. Rule thirty-eight, twenty-nine: ye rule IIS aur CSS officers par nahi lagta, aur Akashvani ya Doordarshan me kaam karne wale bahar ke cadre walon par bhi nahi. Prasar Bharati wale isi clause me aate hain. Rule thirty-seven me aisa carve-out nahi, wahan BSNL aur MTNL ka exception hai.

> REMEMBER: 51% disinvestment needs safeguards, and Rule 38 does not touch IIS or CSS officers in Akashvani and Doordarshan.

## CHAPTER 29 • FIVE FLAGS NOW CLOSED
**FIVE 'NOT VERIFIED' FLAGS — NOW SETTLED BY THE GAZETTE TEXT**  (AUDIT • FLAGS CLOSED)

Q: Which open flags from the first build can now be closed, and what does the rule actually say?

### 37(2) • TWO CLOCKS, NOT ONE
Rule 37(2), verbatim: the PSU frames its rules and regulations within a time frame not exceeding five years. After such rules are framed, all Government servants on deemed deputation shall be asked, within a period not exceeding three months from the date of notification of the rules, to exercise their option to revert to the Government or to seek permanent absorption. Such servants shall be asked to exercise this option within a period of three months from the date of the communication asking them to exercise the option. So: five years to make rules, three months to ask, three months to answer. Rule 38(2) merges the same three clocks into one sub-rule.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Pehla flag band. Rule thirty-seven, two me ek nahi, do clocks hain. PSU ko apne rules notify hone ke teen mahine ke andar staff se option maangna hai, aur staff ko us communication se teen mahine milte hain. Pehle confusion yahi thi ki teen mahine kab se ginte hain.

### 39(4) • BOTH AGES GO WITH THE FILE
Rule 39(4), verbatim: the medical authority shall also be supplied by the Head of Office or Head of Department with a statement of what appears from official records to be the age of the applicant, and if a service book is being maintained for the applicant, the age recorded therein should be reported, and a copy of the letter requesting examination by the medical authority shall be endorsed to the Government servant. So both ages travel, and the servant gets a copy. This matters because invalid pension is age-linked at the margins.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Doosra flag band. Rule thirty-nine, four: medical authority ko official records wali age, aur service book maintain ho to uski age bhi, dono bhejni hain, aur request letter ki copy employee ko. To dono ages jati hain, sirf ek nahi.

### 39(9) • BEFORE OR AFTER APPOINTMENT
Rule 39(9), verbatim: a Government servant who retires even before completing qualifying service of ten years shall also be granted invalid pension, calculated at fifty per cent of emoluments or average emoluments, whichever is more beneficial, in accordance with Rule 44. Proviso: the servant (a) has been examined by the appropriate medical authority EITHER BEFORE his appointment OR AFTER his appointment to the Government service and declared fit by such authority for Government service; AND (b) fulfils all other conditions mentioned in Rule 39. Both limbs are required; only the timing of the fit certificate is flexible.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Teesra flag band. Rule thirty-nine, nine ka proviso: fit certificate appointment se pehle ka ho ya baad ka, dono chalega. Lekin doosri condition bhi chahiye, rule ki baaki saari conditions poori hon. Sirf timing flexible hai.

### 40(2) • ORIGINAL, APPELLATE, REVIEW
Rule 40(2), verbatim: whenever in the case of a Government servant the President passes an order (whether original, appellate or in exercise of power of review) awarding a pension less than the full superannuation pension admissible under these rules, the Union Public Service Commission shall be consulted before such order is passed. Explanation: for this sub-rule, 'pension' includes retirement gratuity. So the UPSC door is not limited to the first order.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Chautha flag band. Rule forty, two: President poore se kam pension ka order dein, to UPSC se pehle consult karna hoga, chahe order original ho, appellate ho, ya review me. Aur pension me retirement gratuity bhi shaamil hai.

### THE TRAP • A FLAG IS NOT A FACT
House discipline: 'not verified' never means 'wrong', it means the wording was not yet seen. Five flags are now closed against the verbatim Gazette text: the three clocks of 37(2), the two ages of 39(4), the either-timing fit certificate of 39(9), the three order-types of 40(2), and the 2026 proviso to Rule 37(7). One caution remains and must still be dated in an answer: the Rs 9,000 floor, the Rs 1,25,000 ceiling and the Rs 54,000 medical-board threshold, because the latest amending O.M. has not been re-read.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: not verified ka matlab galat nahi hota, bas wording dekhi nahi thi. Chaar flags ab band hain. Do khule hain: G.S.R. seven hundred seventy-five E ki exact date, aur rupaye ke figures, kyunki latest O.M. abhi nahi padha. Answer sheet me figure ke saath date likhein.

> REMEMBER: two clocks in 37(2), both ages in 39(4), before OR after appointment in 39(9), three order-types in 40(2), and the 2026 proviso for ex-OFB opt-outs.

## CHAPTER 30 • RULES 35, 36, 37 AND 38 ON ONE GRID
**FOUR ABSORPTION RULES, ONE GRID: WHO MOVES, WHO PAYS, WHO FORFEITS**  (RULES 35 • 36 • 37 • 38)

Q: Can you put all four absorption routes side by side so the differences stop blurring?

### ONE SERVANT AGAINST A WHOLE DEPARTMENT
Rules 35 and 36 are individual: a servant permitted to be absorbed in a State Government, or in a Corporation, Company or Body. The trigger is his own move and his resignation is accepted from the date preceding joining. Rules 37 and 38 are collective: a Department of the Central Government is converted into a PSU or a Central Autonomous Body, and every servant of that Department is transferred en-masse on deemed deputation until absorption. This single difference explains every other difference on the grid.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Sabse bada farq. Rules thirty-five aur thirty-six individual hain, ek banda State ya body me absorb hota hai, uska resignation joining se ek din pehle accept hota hai. Rules thirty-seven aur thirty-eight collective hain, poora department PSU ya autonomous body ban jata hai, sab deemed deputation par.

### WHO PAYS UNDER OPTION (b)
Under option (b) the money moves differently in each rule. Rule 35(7): the State pays pension and gratuity for the entire service including the Central spell, and the Centre bears no proportionate liability. Rule 36(10): the Government discharges its liability by paying in lump sum as a one-time payment, computed on the capitalised value plus retirement gratuity under the 1981 commutation table. Rules 37 and 38: a Pension Fund in the form of a trust is created, the Government pays its share in lump sum into the Fund, and the PSU or body contributes so the Fund is self-supporting.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Option b me paisa alag alag chalta hai. Rule thirty-five, seven: State poori service ki pension degi, Centre ka share nahi. Rule thirty-six, ten: Government ek one-time lump sum dekar apni liability khatam karti hai, nineteen eighty-one table se. Rules thirty-seven aur thirty-eight: Pension Fund trust banta hai, Government usme lump sum daalti hai.

### THE DEARNESS RELIEF SPLIT
Rule 37(16): an employee who opts for pension on the basis of combined service is also eligible to dearness relief as per the Industrial Dearness Allowance pattern, and Rule 37(15) Explanation bases emoluments and average emoluments on PSU pay on the IDA pattern. Rule 38(15): dearness relief as per the Central Dearness Allowance pattern. Rules 35 and 36 contain no dearness relief clause at all, because the pension there is settled at absorption and the State or body scheme takes over.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Dearness relief ka clause sirf thirty-seven aur thirty-eight me hai. Rule thirty-seven, sixteen: IDA pattern. Rule thirty-eight, fifteen: Central DA pattern. Rules thirty-five aur thirty-six me DR ka koi clause nahi, kyunki wahan pension absorption par settle ho jati hai.

### THE TRAP • NO DEEMING IN 35 AND 36
The deeming provision is narrow. Rule 37(4): a servant who does not exercise any option within the prescribed time is deemed to have opted for permanent absorption in the PSU. Rule 38(3): the same for a body. Rules 35(6) and 36(9) grant an option but contain no consequence for silence, so silence there cannot be converted into consent. And Rule 36(8) adds that no lien is retained and all connections with Government stand severed on absorption.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: silence par absorption sirf thirty-seven, four aur thirty-eight, three me hai. Rules thirty-five aur thirty-six me option hai par silence ka result nahi likha, to wahan chup rehna consent nahi banta. Aur Rule thirty-six, aath: lien nahi bachta.

> REMEMBER: one servant goes by 35 or 36, a whole department goes by 37 or 38; only 37 and 38 have the deeming clause.

## CHAPTER 31 • TWO-THIRDS ON A CHART
**TWO-THIRDS, DRAWN: A FLOOR IN RULE 40 AND A CEILING IN RULE 41**  (CHART • RULES 40 vs 41)

Q: Can one picture fix the difference between a two-thirds floor and a two-thirds ceiling?

### READ THE BARS
Chart assumptions, stated openly so the arithmetic can be checked. Rule 40: the full superannuation pension admissible is Rs 60,000 a month, so the floor is two-thirds, Rs 40,000, and the ceiling is the full Rs 60,000: any amount from Rs 40,000 to Rs 60,000 is lawful, and nothing below Rs 40,000 is. Rule 41: the pension that would have been admissible on superannuation is Rs 36,000, so the ceiling is two-thirds, Rs 24,000, and the floor is the Rule 44 minimum of Rs 9,000: anything from Rs 9,000 to Rs 24,000 may be sanctioned. The two examples use different base pensions on purpose, so that no one can compare the bars directly and think the rules are the same shape.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Chart ke assumptions khul kar. Rule forty: poori superannuation pension sixty hazaar, to floor do-tihai, forty hazaar, aur ceiling poore sixty hazaar. Rule forty-one: jo pension banti thi wo thirty-six hazaar, to ceiling do-tihai, twenty-four hazaar, aur floor Rule forty-four ka minimum nau hazaar.

### RULE 40 IS A BAND
Rule 40(1): a Government servant compulsorily retired as a penalty may be granted, by the authority competent to impose the penalty, pension or retirement gratuity or both at a rate not less than two-thirds and not more than full superannuation pension or gratuity admissible on the date of compulsory retirement. The band has a hard bottom, which is why Rule 40(3) can safely pay a provisional two-thirds at once, and Rule 40(5) then lifts the floor to the Rule 44 minimum if that is higher.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Rule forty ek band hai. Sub-rule one: do-tihai se kam nahi, poore se zyada nahi. Authority band ke andar koi bhi point chun sakti hai. Neeche ki limit hard hai, isliye sub-rule teen turant provisional do-tihai de sakta hai.

### RULE 41 IS A LID
Rule 41(1): a servant dismissed or removed forfeits pension and gratuity. Proviso: the authority competent to dismiss or remove may, if the case is deserving of special consideration, sanction a compassionate allowance not exceeding two-thirds of pension or gratuity or both which would have been admissible had he retired on superannuation pension. Rule 41(6): the allowance shall not be less than the minimum pension under Rule 44. So the same fraction caps instead of floors, and the discretion runs downward from it, not upward.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Rule forty-one lid hai. Sub-rule one ka proviso: do-tihai se zyada nahi. Discretion neeche ki taraf chalta hai, upar nahi. Aur sub-rule six floor lagata hai, Rule forty-four ka minimum. To yahan do-tihai maximum hai, guaranteed amount nahi.

### THE TRAP • SAME FRACTION, OPPOSITE JOB
The examination failure is not arithmetic, it is direction. In Rule 40 two-thirds is the worst case that the law still permits; in Rule 41 it is the best case that the law permits. A single sentence in an answer settles it: 'Rule 40 fixes a floor of two-thirds; Rule 41 fixes a ceiling of two-thirds.' Add the shared safety net, Rule 44's minimum pension, which applies through 40(5) and 41(6) to both.

*Spoken (Hinglish):* Trap: do-tihai yaad rakhne wali sankhya nahi, ek direction hai. Rule forty me wo sabse kharab allowed case hai, Rule forty-one me sabse behtar. Answer me ek line likhein: Rule forty floor, Rule forty-one ceiling. Aur dono par Rule forty-four ka minimum.

> REMEMBER: Rule 40 builds UP from two-thirds, Rule 41 caps DOWN at two-thirds, and both sit above the Rule 44 minimum.
