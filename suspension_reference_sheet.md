## Preamble and A

### 3. What suspension is: temporary withdrawal of duties, not a penalty
*SECTION A · WHAT SUSPENSION IS*

> **OM 02.01.2014 (definition):** Suspension, in the context of disciplinary proceedings, may be defined as temporary withdrawal of duties from a government servant, pending inquiry into his/her conduct, with simultaneous reduction in pay and withdrawal of some rights/privileges.
- **Not a penalty:** Suspension, though not a penalty, is to be resorted to sparingly. The government loses services, pays for no work, and the order carries a stigma (Para 3).
> Guide Section A cites OM 11012/17/2013-Estt.(A) dated 02.01.2014, Para 3. Verified: the wording matches Para 3.

### 4. The three grounds for suspension under Rule 10(1), with the guide's labels mapped
*SECTION B · RULE 10(1) GROUNDS*


**Rule 10(1) grounds: rule letter is what goes on the order**

| Rule clause | Ground (in brief) | Guide label | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10(1)(a) | Disciplinary proceeding contemplated or pending | (a) | VERIFIED |
| 10(1)(aa) | Engaged in activities prejudicial to security of the State, in the authority's opinion | (b) | VERIFIED |

### 5. The ground decides the hard cap later: 270 days or two years
*SECTION B · WHICH CLOCK APPLIES*

- Flow: Identify the ground: 10(1)(a), 10(1)(aa) or 10(1)(b) → Record the rule clause in the suspension order → No charge-sheet: cap 270 days for 10(1)(a) → No charge-sheet: cap 2 years for 10(1)(aa) or 10(1)(b) → Deemed suspension under 10(2): cap 2 years from release or intimation
- **Why it matters:** The cap depends on the ground recorded at the start. A wrong ground at the start is a wrong cap later (see Section H).

## Section G

### 21. Reasons for suspension must be communicated within three months if not in the order
*SECTION G · REASONS WITHIN THREE MONTHS*

> **OM 02.01.2014, Para 5:** Reasons for Suspension, if not indicated in the suspension order itself, should be communicated within three months.
- **Best practice:** Write the grounds in the suspension order itself. Silence starts a three-month clock that ends in a stronger challenge.
> Worked example: a 1 Feb order is safe if reasons go by 30 April (or 1 May; convention NOT VERIFIED). Use the earlier date.

### 26. Why timely review matters: courts' criticism of prolonged suspension
*SECTION H · COURTS' CONCERN · OM 18.11.2014*

- **Courts:** Prolonged suspension leaves the officer under hardship, the Government pays subsistence without work, and full salary may follow exoneration.
- **Instruction:** Timely, just review; expeditious finalisation of proceedings. OM 11012/17/2013-Estt.(A) dated 18.11.2014 (the 2022 document cites this date before Section I).
